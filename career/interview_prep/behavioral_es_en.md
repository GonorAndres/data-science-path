# Behavioral Interview Prep (ES + EN)

Behavioral rounds screen for communication, collaboration, and judgment. Every DS loop in Mexico has at least one behavioral round, usually with the hiring manager.

## The STAR framework

Answer behavioral questions in the STAR format:

- **S**ituation: the context (one or two sentences).
- **T**ask: what you were responsible for.
- **A**ction: what you specifically did (lead with "I", not "we").
- **R**esult: the measurable outcome.

Keep each answer under 3 minutes. Interviewers get bored at 4.

## Preparation workflow

1. Prepare 6 to 8 STAR stories from your real experience (internship, project, academic, volunteer).
2. Each story should cover at least two of these themes: conflict, failure, leadership, learning, collaboration, ambiguity, prioritization, impact.
3. Practice telling each story out loud in under 3 minutes, in both Spanish and English.
4. Map each question below to one or two of your stories.

## Common questions (Spanish + English)

### 1. Tell me about a time you made a mistake.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre un momento en que cometiste un error."

**EN:** "Tell me about a time you made a mistake."

**What they look for:** Self-awareness, recovery, learning.

**Structure:**
- Concrete mistake (technical or judgment).
- What you did to mitigate.
- What you changed in your process.
- Do not dwell on blame; stay action-focused.

**Trap:** "I worked too hard" is not a mistake. Interviewers know the cliche. Pick a real one.

---

### 2. Tell me about a time you had a conflict with a teammate.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre un conflicto con un companero de equipo."

**EN:** "Tell me about a conflict with a teammate."

**What they look for:** Professionalism, empathy, resolution.

**Structure:**
- Describe the disagreement (technical or process; never personal).
- Describe both sides fairly.
- Describe your action: listening, proposing compromise, escalating appropriately.
- Describe the outcome and what you learned.

**Trap:** Do not trash the other person. Even if they were wrong, the interviewer hears how you talk about colleagues.

---

### 3. Tell me about a project that failed.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre un proyecto que fracaso."

**EN:** "Tell me about a project that failed."

**What they look for:** Accountability, learning, resilience.

**Structure:**
- Clear definition of the failure (missed deadline, model underperformed, business did not adopt).
- Root cause analysis (what went wrong, with honesty).
- Your contribution to the failure (own your part).
- Specific changes you made since.

**Trap:** "It failed because of reasons outside my control" signals lack of ownership.

---

### 4. Tell me about a time you had to work with incomplete information.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre un momento en que trabajaste con informacion incompleta."

**EN:** "Tell me about a time you had to work with incomplete information."

**What they look for:** Prioritization, decision-making under uncertainty.

**Structure:**
- Describe the information you had and what was missing.
- What assumptions did you make and why.
- How did you validate those assumptions as you went.
- Outcome.

---

### 5. Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology quickly.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre un momento en que aprendiste una tecnologia nueva rapido."

**EN:** "Tell me about a time you learned a new technology quickly."

**What they look for:** Learning agility, self-motivation.

**Structure:**
- The context (why you had to learn it).
- The resources you used.
- The timeline.
- The measurable outcome (you shipped X with it).

---

### 6. Tell me about a time you persuaded someone.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre un momento en que persuadiste a alguien."

**EN:** "Tell me about a time you persuaded someone."

**What they look for:** Communication, influence without authority.

**Structure:**
- What you were advocating for and why.
- The audience and their initial stance.
- Your approach (data, demo, story).
- The decision and outcome.

---

### 7. Tell me about a time you prioritized poorly.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre un momento en que priorizaste mal."

**EN:** "Tell me about a time you prioritized poorly."

**What they look for:** Self-reflection on judgment.

**Structure:**
- The decision (which task you chose over another).
- Why it was the wrong call in hindsight.
- What you changed.

---

### 8. Tell me about your proudest accomplishment.

**ES:** "Cuentame sobre tu logro del que mas orgulloso estas."

**EN:** "Tell me about your proudest accomplishment."

**What they look for:** What motivates you.

**Structure:**
- The achievement.
- Why it was difficult.
- Your specific contribution.
- Why it matters to you.

---

### 9. Why this company?

**ES:** "Por que esta empresa?"

**EN:** "Why this company?"

**What they look for:** You did your homework and you have a real reason.

**Structure:**
- One specific thing about the company (product, team, mission) that resonates with you.
- How it connects to what you want to work on.
- Why this stage of your career.

**Trap:** Generic answers ("great culture, good people") land flat. Name something concrete.

---

### 10. Why should we hire you?

**ES:** "Por que deberiamos contratarte?"

**EN:** "Why should we hire you?"

**What they look for:** Self-awareness about what you bring.

**Structure:**
- Three concrete strengths with evidence (project, result, skill).
- Acknowledge one area where you want to grow.
- Tie your strengths to what the role needs.

---

### 11. Tell me about feedback you received that was hard to hear.

**What they look for:** Receptiveness to criticism, willingness to change.

**Structure:**
- The feedback (specific, ideally negative).
- Your initial reaction (honest: defensive, surprised, hurt).
- What you did with it.
- How your behavior changed.

---

### 12. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.

**What they look for:** Ability to push back professionally, respect for hierarchy.

**Structure:**
- The disagreement (technical or strategic).
- How you raised it.
- Whether your view prevailed, and either way, how you executed on the final decision.

---

## Mexican-specific behavioral notes

- **Hierarchy awareness.** Mexican workplaces often have stronger hierarchy expectations than US peer companies. Do not present a story where you publicly overruled your manager; frame as "I shared a counter-proposal, we discussed, the final call was theirs, and I executed."
- **Usted vs tu.** In interviews with senior managers at traditional firms (banks, insurance), default to usted until invited otherwise. At startups (Nu, Bitso, Kavak) tu is the norm.
- **Humility signaling.** Overly confident self-promotion reads as unseemly in traditional MX companies. Balance achievement with what you learned.

## Plantillas de historias (ES, para que practiques)

### Historia 1: Error tecnico

```
Situacion: Durante el proyecto de [nombre], trabajando en [empresa/equipo], descubri que...
Tarea: Mi responsabilidad era...
Accion: Yo [verbo en pasado + que hiciste]. Primero [accion 1], luego [accion 2], y finalmente [accion 3].
Resultado: El impacto fue [metrica cuantificada]. Ademas aprendi [leccion]. Desde entonces, aplico [cambio en proceso].
```

### Historia 2: Conflicto resuelto

```
Situacion: En el equipo de [contexto], tenia una diferencia de opinion con [rol del companero] sobre [tema].
Tarea: Necesitaba [objetivo].
Accion: Yo propuse una sesion breve de [N minutos] para alinear. Escuche su punto de [resumir su vista], y luego expuse mi posicion con [datos/ejemplo]. Llegamos a [acuerdo].
Resultado: El resultado fue [metrica o outcome]. Aprendi que [leccion].
```

### Historia 3: Aprendizaje rapido

```
Situacion: El proyecto requeria [tecnologia] que yo no conocia.
Tarea: Tenia [plazo] para tener algo funcional.
Accion: Yo invierti [N horas/dias] en [recursos especificos: libro, curso, documentacion]. Construi [proyecto chico de practica] en los primeros N dias, y luego integre al codigo del equipo.
Resultado: El feature quedo listo [a tiempo/adelantado] y [metrica de impacto]. El aprendizaje se ha quedado conmigo.
```

## Questions to ask the interviewer

Have 4 to 6 questions ready. Good ones:

- How is success measured for this role in the first six months?
- What is the most ambiguous or open problem your team is working on?
- How does the team balance new-model work vs maintenance?
- How do you onboard new data scientists? Mentorship structure?
- What is the team's tech stack and how much of it will I touch day-to-day?
- What would surprise me about working here?

Weak questions (skip):

- What is your company culture like? (vague; you can read it on Glassdoor)
- What are the growth opportunities? (self-interested; ask about team growth instead)
- Do you offer remote work? (search the job ad)

## Red flags to watch for

During the interview, note if:

- The manager cannot clearly describe what success looks like in 6 months.
- The team size keeps changing in the narrative ("we had 12, then 6, then 8").
- No mention of a hiring plan (are you a replacement or net new?).
- Product roadmap is hazy.
- "We move fast" is used to justify not having a clear process.

## Post-interview

Within 24 hours, send the thank-you note (templates in `recruiter_outreach_templates.md`). After a decision is made (positive or negative), debrief yourself:

- What questions caught you off guard?
- What stories landed well?
- What would you change for next time?

Keep a personal log. The behavioral interview is a skill that compounds with practice.
