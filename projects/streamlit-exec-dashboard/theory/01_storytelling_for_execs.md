# Storytelling for Execs

## The inverted pyramid

Write and design like a newspaper front page:

1. **Headline.** One sentence: the key finding.
2. **Lead.** The chart or KPI that anchors the headline.
3. **Supporting evidence.** Two or three related charts.
4. **Background.** Drill-down tables, segmentation.
5. **Appendix.** Methodology, data sources.

Execs read the top. Analysts scroll for the rest.

## The "so what" test

For every chart: can you write a one-line conclusion under it that a decision-maker could act on?

- Good: "Revenue growth accelerated 12% MoM, driven by new customer acquisition in Sao Paulo."
- Bad: "Revenue chart by month."

If you cannot write the "so what", the chart does not belong.

## Numeric formatting

- Round where the precision does not matter. "R$ 1.2M" not "R$ 1,184,293.47".
- Consistent date formats. Always YYYY-MM or Mon YYYY, not a mix.
- Use unit suffixes: K, M, B.
- Percent changes signed: "+12%" or "-3%", not just "12%".

## Color semantics

- Green for good, red for bad. Culturally standard in Mexico and most markets.
- Colorblind-safe palettes: Viridis, Okabe-Ito.
- Avoid rainbow scales; they imply ordering that rarely exists.
- Use one accent color per page, not three.

## Sparklines and microvisuals

A KPI tile is more useful with a tiny trend line underneath the number. Shows "yes this is good AND the trend is up" without extra real estate.

## Callouts

Pin annotations to important points:

- "Launch of new feature" on a retention chart.
- "Marketing campaign ran" on a spike in acquisitions.
- Black Friday on a revenue spike.

Callouts prevent misinterpretation.
