# Problem Framing: Competition Log

## The goal

Not to win. To:

- Exercise the full ML cycle against an external, evaluated benchmark.
- Produce a public artifact (submissions, notebooks, writeup) that demonstrates discipline.
- Learn from top-performing solutions after the competition ends.

A bronze medal signals the 10 percent of participants who knew what they were doing. A non-medal finish with a clear writeup and public notebooks still signals professionalism.

## Picking a competition

Good choices:

- Tabular problems (gradient boosting fits, manageable compute).
- NLP or CV if you have GPU access.
- Time series if forecasting interests you.

Avoid for a first-serious-attempt:

- Competitions with leak-prone public LBs (well-known "shake-up" competitions).
- Very long competitions (3+ months) unless you can commit.
- Heavily team-oriented competitions where solo attempts are uncompetitive.

## Time budget

Plan 20 to 40 hours total. Track actual time spent; it is almost always more than planned.

- Week 1: EDA, baseline, study winning solutions from past similar competitions.
- Week 2: feature engineering, experimentation.
- Week 3: tuning, ensembling.
- Final week: polish, writeup.

## Success metrics

- Valid submission.
- At least 5 submissions (shows iteration).
- Public LB ranking in the top 50 percent.
- Public notebook with upvotes (optional but valuable).
- Writeup with clear takeaways.
